## M7 — Depth & Polish

- [x] **Unified body viewer** (step 1 of the single universe renderer): planet + surface merged — solid worlds are streamed terrain at every altitude, envelopes are shader spheres, everything else (sun, moons, eclipses, rings, atmosphere, backdrop) shared in one scene.

- [x] **Single universe renderer, step 3**: galaxy view folded in — the 30 pc neighborhood rides in the scene as true 3D points that double as the night sky's near field (the backdrop keeps only far shells and the Milky Way glow), so every view is a preset of the one renderer and a single wheel ride runs from interstellar space to any planet's ground. Travel to a neighbor rebuilds the system in place. Open: whole-disk galaxy rendering beyond the neighborhood (clusters/nebulae land with the M6 leftovers).
- [x] Clouds over terrain: a translucent deck shell above the highest terrain, sharing the shader-sphere planets' drifting cloud field (the focus view keeps the climate the far view promised), with close-range detail fade-in and a pass-through fade. Open: cloud shadows on the ground; volumetric decks stay a later item.
- [x] Black-hole lensing: the galactic nucleus is drawn by tracing one null geodesic per pixel through the Schwarzschild metric (the Binet equation in Cartesian form — exact, no tuned bend factor), so the shadow at 3√3 r_g, the photon ring, the Einstein rings of the nuclear cluster and the accretion flow's far side lifted over the top of the hole are all one integration. The flow's temperature profile, thickness and optical depth are the model's; colour is the blackbody table read at the Doppler- and gravity-shifted temperature each patch is seen at, so the beaming asymmetry is δ⁴ rather than a dialled constant. σT⁴ across a flow runs five decades for a starved torus and eleven for a fed disc, further than a screen reaches, so the *source* alone is compressed under a display gamma derived from its own slope, to r^−1; what reaches the eye falls faster wherever the flow has thinned, because a thinning column emits less of what it holds. Nor is a disc opaque everywhere: its column is its surface density and that falls away with radius, so only the innermost disc is a surface and past a few inner radii it is gas the sky shows through — which is also what lets the clumping decide which lanes are shut and which are open. The model's cold disc reaches its true self-gravity edge thousands of r_g out, but the picture is drawn to a dozen inner radii, which holds five sixths of the light: the cold decades past that are an opaque plane carrying a few percent of the luminosity over nearly all of the area, and drawn they leave the hole a speck on a lit floor — which is why no photograph, render or GRMHD simulation shows them either. The camera arrives outside the flow it is looking at, for the same reason. The drawn edge is thinned in substance rather than in brightness — emission and opacity together — so the flow dissolves into the sky instead of ending in an opaque dark rim it does not have: at the radius drawn it is still forty thousand kelvin, and even at its true edge two thousand six hundred. The flow is not smooth: the magnetorotational instability that lets it accrete at all leaves the gas clumped on a log-normal density distribution of the width simulations measure, and the shear draws every clump into a trailing filament — so the structure is the instability's, sampled in co-rotating coordinates, not noise laid over a disc. The lensed background is the sky captured from the hole itself, so every star gets bent — inside the footprint where bending is worth what the capture's resolution costs, and no further, or a sharp star field would go soft the moment the camera crossed the boundary. Sprites drawn into that capture are scaled by its angular resolution against the screen's, since a point sprite is sized in pixels and would otherwise come back out of it fatter than the same star drawn straight to the screen beside it. Propagation is Kerr: Carter's separated equations in Mino time, exact, with each ray's constants read off the tetrad of a real observer at the camera rather than a flat-space approximation to it — which is the step that decides whether the answer is a Kerr shadow or a Schwarzschild one wearing a correction. Integrated over thousands of rays, the boundary between what falls and what escapes reproduces Bardeen's critical curve to a fraction of a percent at every spin, and the rendered silhouette agrees with the closed form pixel for pixel to sub-pixel accuracy; the deflection at large impact parameter carries the second-order 15π/4b² that separates general relativity from a Newtonian argument with the right factor of two. So the shadow is a circle of 3√3 only when the hole is still, and dragged into a D and offset from the ring at spin — 4.83 r_g looking down the axis of a Thorne-limited hole against 5.196 for a static one. The flow now reaches its own inner edge at any spin, because matter inside the last stable orbit is given the plunging geodesic that leaves it carrying the energy and angular momentum it had there: no floor, no free parameter, and the radial term switches itself off above the boundary because a circular orbit's own constants make it vanish. A thick flow is passed through rather than crossed — a starved hole's ion torus is half as deep as it is wide and translucent, so what reaches the eye is the path integral through it, which is why the ring is where the sightline runs longest through the plasma rather than where the material is. Stellar-mass holes get the same pass, and what they look like is decided by the company they keep: a hole is fed by whatever companion is close enough to lose material to it, through the wind it sheds (Bondi–Hoyle–Lyttleton capture, off a Reimers or line-driven mass-loss rate, at a speed the beta law says the gas has actually reached by the time it arrives) or through its Roche lobe once the star's own radius exceeds Eggleton's, on its thermal time if it is the heavier and its far slower nuclear one if it is not. Below a percent of Eddington the flow cannot cool as fast as it falls and its efficiency drops with its supply, so the light goes as the square of the rate — which is the whole reason a hole with nothing but interstellar gas to eat is not a catalogued class of object. Nearly all of them are exactly that, and are drawn as what they are: a shadow, a photon ring, and the star field bent into an Einstein ring around it, with no disc at all. The turbulence is not frozen: it advects with the flow's own rotation, shears differentially, and re-forms, since an MRI eddy turns over in about an orbit and at the innermost orbit here that is forty seconds. Two realisations run half a lifetime out of phase, each reseeded while it carries no weight and blended so the variance rather than the mean is preserved — the same construction the gas-giant deck uses, and for the same reason: one frozen field advected forever shears into threads that never renew. The bulk rotation is applied to both alike and never resets, so the flow's turning stays continuous through the handover. It shows on a thin disc, where the clumping is read at a single crossing; a hot torus is a path integral through a hundred decorrelated cells and averages its own fluctuations away, which is why one looks filamentary and the other smooth. Jets were looked at and not built, for a reason about scale rather than difficulty. A spinning hole threaded by a magnetically arrested flow does drive one — the Blandford–Znajek power follows from the spin and the flux GRMHD measures the arrested state saturating at, and comes out above unity in efficiency for the spin this nucleus has — but almost none of that power radiates near the hole. It is Poynting flux, and it becomes visible only where it dissipates, which for M87 is hundreds to thousands of gravitational radii out. Inside the dozen inner radii the flow is drawn to, a correctly modelled jet is nearly invisible, and making it show would mean exaggerating it. A jet is a different picture at a different framing — one where the shadow is a speck — rather than an addition to this one. Open: that wider framing; the black-hole X-ray binaries the physics is ready for are absent because a hole's companion is drawn at 0.15–0.95 of the progenitor's mass and so is always another massive star, dead by the time the hole exists.
