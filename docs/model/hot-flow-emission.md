# Hot-torus emission

For the broader scientific explanation and a guide to the visible features, start with [Black holes: science, model, and appearance](black-holes.md). This page describes the detailed plasma implementation.

The hot flow has its own plasma spectrum. Its former luminosity/area effective temperature remains in the accretion data model for compatibility, but no longer determines the torus color or emission. Thin disks retain their Planck spectrum.

## Physical closure

Steady inflow fixes electron density from the mass supply, a Gaussian scale height, and radial velocity `alpha c (H/R)^2 / sqrt(r/rg)`, with alpha = 0.1. Ions have a virial temperature; electrons have a prescribed radial temperature profile, initially capped at 30 billion K. Magnetic pressure is one tenth of gas pressure (beta = 10).

Electrons comprise a Maxwell–Jüttner core and a cooled power-law tail (index 3.5, maximum Lorentz factor 100,000). The tail receives 1% of electron kinetic energy while conserving particle count. These are fixed modeling assumptions, not measured parameters for each generated nucleus.

Continuous synchrotron emission integrates the synchrotron kernel over this population. Thermal absorption follows Kirchhoff's law; tail absorption uses the distribution derivative. A representative isotropic pitch angle replaces pitch-angle integration. Electron-ion and electron-electron bremsstrahlung use relativistic cooling fits with an exponential spectral shape. Up to three local Compton source iterations redistribute seed photons. Direct synchrotron and free-free emissivity remain intact: the ray tracer applies extinction once, rather than attenuating the direct source in the table and again along the ray.

For each representative column, total extinction is true absorption plus electron scattering. Its escape probability is `(1-exp(-tau))/tau`; the remaining probability divides between absorption and scattering according to their extinction fractions. Each order feeds the next through the scattering fraction. Redistribution deposits photon number between neighbouring log-frequency bins with weights linear in frequency, preserving the prescribed mean photon energy. Tests explicitly account for photons escaping, absorbed, shifted outside the finite band, or left in the truncated scattering remainder. Three orders remain a low-optical-depth approximation; the remainder is not silently reassigned to escaping light.

The reduced mean frequency gain is `A = 1 + 4 theta + 16 theta²`, with a recoil turnover `nu_out = nu A / (1 + (A-1) h nu / (3 kT))`. This is a deterministic representative shift, not the broad exact thermal redistribution kernel. Scattering opacity in the bolometric closure uses the Klein–Nishina total cross section evaluated at a representative electron-frame energy `h nu (1+3 theta)/(m_e c²)`. The factor is a thermal closure, not a full distribution average. Optical ray extinction retains the Thomson limit. Recoil replaces the old exponential high-energy cutoff, which discarded photons. The existing electron-temperature and radiative-budget guards bound the energy supplied to the radiation.

Forty-eight radial shells sample 256 frequencies from 10 MHz to 10^23 Hz. Estimated escaping bolometric power uses a representative vertical escape probability. If that exceeds the accretion model's radiative budget, a bounded search lowers the electron temperature profile. A residual source cap applies if the 500 million K floor still exceeds the budget. The model never raises luminosity to fill the budget. Reabsorbed internal synchrotron emission is not counted again as net cooling.

## Rendering and cost

Visible radiance is CIE-integrated after applying `g^3 j_nu(nu/g)`. The original radial 48 × 64 table remains a 60 KiB reference for validation. The animated torus uses an explicit local-state table: 16 radii × 46 frequency shifts × 225 combinations of density, electron temperature, magnetic field and tail energy fraction. It stores two banks, log RGB emissivity and log RGB absorption, in a 46 × 32 × 225 3-D float texture (5,299,200 bytes). The banks share each state layer, keeping the depth below the WebGL2 guaranteed minimum limit of 256. Hardware interpolation handles temperature as well as radius and frequency shift; eight samples interpolate the other three plasma variables. The torus material lookup does not change geometric precision or output resolution; polar outflow sampling is described below. A small CPU cache avoids rebuilding identical tables; each renderer owns and disposes its GPU textures.

All hot flows use the same display reference of 10^11 erg/s/cm²/sr, followed by the existing scene exposure and HDR transform. This is camera calibration chosen for readable images, not a claim of naked-eye visibility. There is no per-flow optical peak normalization. Density and magnetic field, the spectral slope, and relativistic shifts determine color and asymmetry; a weakly supplied flow may have little visible emission despite very hot electrons.

The renderer caches local spectral interpolation once per changed material state in a 96 × 2944 RGBA16F target (2,260,992 bytes), covering the 3072 spatial cells at 46 frequency shifts. Each ray segment interpolates two cached shift slices per bank instead of evaluating eight plasma-state corners per bank. Camera-only movement reuses this material cache. This adds one small GPU pass on animated frames and does not change the ray-trace or output resolution. The diagnostic retains direct evaluation for image comparison; caching introduces additional spatial interpolation error, measured separately from geodesic integration error.

The transfer retains the existing Kerr geometry and mean Doppler velocity. True absorption is sampled at representative red, green and blue wavelengths (610, 550 and 460 nm), shifted to the emitter frame independently for each medium. RGB transmission and the exact homogeneous-cell formal solution then attenuate both background and emitted light by color. This is a diagonal three-band approximation applied to CIE-integrated RGB, not full spectral transfer or a dust reddening model. Thomson scattering adds grey extinction in the optical limit. Local changes affect both the spectrum and absorption, replacing the former density-squared brightness multiplier with a frozen mean spectrum.

## Asynchronous preparation

The application uses a streaming black-hole owner with a stable scene group. It displays a flow-free shadow and the actual lensed sky immediately, then installs the emitting flow when its worker-built tables and asynchronously compiled shaders are ready. Equilibrium, local spectra, and wind/jet spectra run in a short-lived module worker under the shared generation scheduler. Typed buffers are transferred to the main thread; CPU spectral generation is never used as a blocking fallback if the worker fails.

The generation indicator reports queued/equilibrium/plasma/wind/jet/display stages. Leaving the core or a stellar black-hole system terminates active generation, withdraws queued work and ignores stale replies. GPU objects being compiled remain alive until every pending preparation settles. A single completed CPU payload is cached for quick revisits; it is an in-memory cache, not persistence across reloads. Each live renderer owns and disposes its GPU textures. Physical readouts refresh once the accepted model arrives.

Main-thread construction, texture upload and driver preparation still require short intervals of work; moving the expensive integrals off-thread does not guarantee a zero-hitch frame. The diagnostic records frames rendered during preparation, maximum preparation frame interval and observed main-thread long tasks separately from steady rendering performance.

## Transport, heating and flares

A 32-radius × 96-azimuth field carries density and specific thermal, magnetic and energetic-electron energies relative to the radial equilibrium. This is a model of vertically coherent columns, not a 3-D fluid simulation. A small vertical twist maps the columns into the existing torus volume. The thin disk uses its own seeded surface-density, heat, and stress field; see [the thin-disk model](black-holes.md#thin-disk).

Conservative upwind transport follows differential rotation and steady inward mass flux, with a periodic azimuth, a replenished outer boundary and a draining inner boundary. Small bounded azimuthal velocity fluctuations compress the field through face fluxes instead of injecting density. Initial perturbations are deterministic from the hole's radius and spin, and their ring-mean density is one. CFL substeps preserve positivity; the existing displayed-orbit clock caps each frame to .02 inner orbits and prevents catch-up backlogs after time jumps. This low-order transport introduces numerical diffusion.

Bounded shear work replenishes a magnetic energy reservoir. Dissipation transfers energy to thermal and energetic electrons; stronger-than-equilibrium fields dissipate faster and allocate a larger fraction to accelerated particles. Each local source update balances supplied work, remaining stored energy and radiated energy exactly, and cannot spend a reservoir below zero. Thermal cooling uses the reference escaping power; the energetic reservoir cools ten times faster in this reduced approximation. Acceleration takes at most 25% of dissipated power, not 25% of all electron energy. Enhancements move with the gas and retain a cooling history rather than appearing as independently timed visual flashes.

The thermal energy-to-temperature response and adiabatic exponent come from the Maxwell–Jüttner core's local heat capacity. Compression also strengthens a tangled frozen-in field as B∝rho^(2/3). These changes select explicitly evaluated spectra, without a painted color ramp. The table covers density, temperature and field ratios .7–1.4 and tail-energy-fraction ratios .3–3; values outside the domain saturate in the material lookup while the reservoirs continue evolving. Five temperature and magnetic-field nodes resolve more of the spectral curvature than three nodes. Representative simultaneous changes are tested against direct emission calculations with a 20% per-visible-band tolerance; this is not a universal error bound or precision spectroscopy.

A CPU interpolation of the same local states estimates escaping bolometric power over the whole field. It can attenuate emission to the common radiative budget and never brightens it or normalizes its optical peak. This global estimate still uses representative vertical escape. The perturbation transport assumes a steady radial background, so the local source ledger does not establish an exact global gravitational/enthalpy energy budget.

## Inner winds and jet bases

The hot-flow accretion rate continues to mean gas reaching the horizon, preserving `L = eta Mdot_h c²`. Above 8 gravitational radii the inward supply rises as `Mdot(r) ∝ r^s`, with a default requested `s = .35`. Gas launched into the jet is added smoothly between its launch radius and twice that radius. Outer supply is exactly horizon accretion plus wind loss plus jet loading. The density and Thomson extinction use this radial supply, and perturbation transport removes local specific reservoirs with the lost mass. The wind exponent decreases if its energy requirement would exceed half the remaining binding-energy budget `(eta_thin - eta_hot) Mdot_h c²`; it approaches zero at the thin-disc transition.

For each launched wind mass element the reduced budget uses binding work `c²/(2r_launch)`, terminal kinetic energy `.5 (.25²) c²/r_launch`, magnetic power `.05 c²/r_launch`, and electron heating `.20 c²/r_launch`. The cumulative launch-energy integral supplies the mass-weighted terminal velocity. The wind angular mass flux is `7.5 mu² (1-mu²)`, normalized over the sphere, so the wind occupies broad surface layers around the torus rather than filling its axis uniformly.

Jet power uses the Blandford–Znajek fit `P = .044/(4pi) phi_BH² Omega_H² (1+1.38 Omega_H²-9.2 Omega_H⁴) Mdot_h c²`, where `Omega_H = a/(2r_H)`. Spin without horizon magnetic flux does not guarantee a jet. Diagnostics default to dimensionless flux 10; generated nuclei draw flux log-uniformly from 3–35 with a separate seed, preserving all existing nucleus properties. This population prior is an explicit assumption, not a measured distribution. Jet power draws on spin energy, with 65% allocated to terminal kinetic power, 30% to magnetic power and 5% to electrons. The terminal Lorentz factor is 3; the prescribed acceleration approaches it over 8 gravitational radii. Spin evolution, magnetic-flux transport and launch dynamics are not solved.

Both polar lobes use a normalized profile `exp[-(1-|mu|)/w] / [w (1-exp(-1/w))]`, with `w = .18²/2`. The mass density follows `Mdot h(mu) / (4pi r² Gamma v m_p)` and the comoving field follows the corresponding Poynting flux. These are quasi-spherical continuity closures interpreted locally, not exact covariant GRMHD continuity. Outflow four-velocities are radial in the locally non-rotating frame, including Kerr frame dragging. Each medium receives its own photon frequency shift and comoving path length. Light and optical depths are summed within a transfer cell before applying attenuation, avoiding an artificial order between overlapping torus, wind and jet gas. Wind launch angular momentum beyond frame dragging is omitted.

Wind electrons start at 60% of the reference local torus electron temperature. Jet electrons start at up to 30 billion K and cool as `r^-.7`; both are limited by allocated electron energy per particle. Tail energy fractions are 1% and 3%, respectively. The same synchrotron, free-free, absorption and local Compton routines determine their spectra. Integrated local emitted power, conservatively including reabsorbed light, is capped at the component electron budget. This ceiling never raises a faint outflow to fill unused power; neither color nor optical brightness is prescribed. The geometry and heating are steady closures, so these outflows do not yet inherit the torus's moving fluctuations.

Two 24-radius × 20-angle × 46-frequency-shift tables each contain emission and extinction banks: linear RGB emission and RGB absorption plus Thomson extinction (1,413,120 bytes total). The ray combines all three media before evaluating the per-color cell transfer. Jet sampling uses its own angular coordinate to resolve the narrow funnel; the tail beyond nine angular e-folds is omitted (under 0.013% of its mass-flux profile). Tables build once per physical model. A further 4 KiB radial table caches the steady wind/jet speeds and inflow supply ratio, avoiding repeated powers and exponentials in each ray segment. Shared Kerr factors are evaluated once before contracting with each outflow speed. Geometric precision varies smoothly toward the polar region. All production rays use a consistent volume quadrature sample every two geometry steps; changing that grouping abruptly near a jet creates visible seams in the surrounding torus. Negligible torus spectra are skipped in its evacuated funnel (vertical density below one millionth of the midplane), while wind/jet transfer continues. Regular versus fine integration checks cover these approximations. The current model ends at the existing 60 gravitational radii and fades through the existing outer volume taper: these are inner jet bases, not galaxy-scale jets. Thin discs do not receive this hot-flow outflow prescription.

(2011)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1745-3933.2011.01147.x) and [McKinney et al. (2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.4163). The numerical closures above are deliberately reduced and are not full reproductions of those simulations.

## Limits

The core camera now meters bright hot tori once, when the worker's reference
spectra arrive. It estimates the brightest vertical column outside 3 rg at
unit frequency shift, including absorption and Thomson extinction, and aims
for display luminance 0.6. Exposure never exceeds the previous 0.08 sky preset,
so a faint flow is not brightened to match a luminous one. This is an approximate
camera framing preset, not eye adaptation or a new plasma energy normalization;
it does not meter viewing angle, turbulence, or jet brightness. All scene light
shares that exposure, including the sky. The setting remains fixed while orbiting
and fades back to the galaxy exposure when leaving the core. Manual compensation
runs from −16 to +4 stops in quarter-stop increments, relative to the preset.

This is a reduced emission and perturbation model, not GRMHD or a self-consistent local electron energy equation. The temperature/budget closure and vertical escape probability do not establish an exact global relativistic luminosity. It omits extended jets, time-dependent outflow dynamics, polarized transfer, cyclotron harmonics, detailed frequency-dependent Gaunt factors, spatial Compton transport and scattered background light. The mean photon Doppler velocity remains the existing circular/plunging prescription; the perturbation field's slow radial and compressive velocities are not added to that tetrad. At high optical depth the three-order local scattering approximation is especially limited. Scattering of the external galaxy sky into a new line of sight remains omitted: an RGB sky cube does not carry the broad seed spectrum needed for hot-electron redistribution. Scattering out of the observed beam is included. The equilibrium tail fraction and reconnection/cooling prescriptions remain uncertain astrophysics, although local energy transfers are explicit.
